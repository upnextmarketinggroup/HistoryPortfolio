Secondary Sources

Evaluating one work of history writing

Source Citation

Lamoreaux, Naomi R. “The Problem of Bigness: From Standard Oil to Google.” Journal of Economic Perspectives, vol. 33, no. 3, 2019, pp. 94–117.

Author Profile

Naomi R. Lamoreaux is an economic and business historian. She was Professor of Economics and History at Yale, chairing the History Department from 2013, and now works at the University of Michigan Law School. She earned her PhD in history from Johns Hopkins in 1979, writing her PhD paper on the great merger movement, and has studied trusts and corporate consolidation ever since.

Event Timeline

  • Late 1800s

    Standard Oil and other trusts dominate industries during the Gilded Age.

  • Early to mid 1900s

    Antitrust law focuses on whether big companies use unfair tactics.

  • Mid to late 1900s

    Focus shifts to how much market share a company controls.

  • Leading up to 2019

    Focus shifts again to whether consumers pay higher prices.

  • 2019

    Lamoreaux publishes the article, using Google and Amazon as the modern case.

Evaluation

Historical Context

Extended analysis

She traces over a century of history, from Gilded Age trusts to today’s tech giants, showing how the legal standard itself changed over time.

Audience

Described

Published in the Journal of Economic Perspectives, an academic journal, so the audience is mostly economists and policymakers.

Purpose

Extended analysis

She argues antitrust policy should refocus on company conduct, backed by historical case evidence.

Point of View

Brief analysis

She writes as an economic historian, which gives her expertise but a narrower economic lens.

Why (Importance)

Extended analysis

It connects a century old legal debate directly to today’s fights over Google and Amazon.

Historical Accuracy

Avoids misconceptions, no errors

She backs her claims with real antitrust cases and legal history, without relying on unsupported opinions.
